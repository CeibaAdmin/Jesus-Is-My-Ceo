01Mission & Vision

Somebody is already in charge of what you lead.

The name isn't a slogan. It's an org chart. Jesus holds the top seat over everything we lead — the vision, the direction, the final call — and we operate one line beneath Him.

Most of us learned to lead by taking ownership. Set the vision, own the outcome, carry the weight. That instinct gets things built, and it also quietly makes us the highest authority in the room. This group exists to correct the chart. You still lead. You just stop pretending it's yours.

Practically, that means we bring real decisions to a table of people who will ask what God is saying before they ask what's easiest or most profitable — and who will follow up next week to see whether you actually listened.

  • Vision

    We seek direction from the One who sees the whole field — and the whole story.

  • Direction

    Decisions submitted in prayer before they're submitted to a spreadsheet.

  • Accountability

    Peers who know your pressures, your temptations, and your commitments.

  • Reporting

    Every leader reports to someone. We report to Christ, out loud, together.

02Our Guiding Principle

Stewardship

Everything is borrowed. God is the ultimate owner of everything we have, had, and will ever have.

If what we lead isn't ours, the pressure changes shape. Decisions stop being about protecting what we built and start being about managing well what we were handed — which frees us to be generous when it's costly, patient when it's slow, and honest when a shortcut would be easier to explain.

It reaches the resources first: the money, the time, and the influence we've been given to steward. Then it reaches ambition, because a steward asks whether an opportunity is his to take before he asks whether he can win it. And it reaches relationships — the people we lead, serve, and work alongside, and the people at this table, are all entrusted to us, not owed to us.

05The Study

Eight sessions, in order.

We work through the Faith Driven Entrepreneur video study across the semester. Each session is a short film and a long conversation.

  1. 01

    Our Call to Create

    Why building and leading anything is itself an act of making, and what that has to do with the God who made things first.

  2. 02

    God Owns My Business

    The session this whole group is built around. Not “God gets ten percent and I keep the rest” — He owns what you lead, and you’re the one stewarding it.

  3. 03

    Don’t Worship Work

    Ambition without contentment. Which risks are wise to take when success was never promised to you.

  4. 04

    Excellence Matters

    Quality as worship — and the point where chasing excellence quietly becomes an obstacle to actually finishing anything.

  5. 05

    Faithful vs. Wilful

    Where your identity actually sits, what habits keep God in front of you, and the uncomfortable question of how you rest.

  6. 06

    Ministry in Word

    Telling the truth about your own story, including the failures — and what God did with them.

  7. 07

    Ministry in Deed

    The culture you have actually built, and whether the people you lead, serve, and work alongside can tell the difference.

  8. 08

    Global Movement

    Your company, team, or ministry as a place people meet Jesus, often without noticing — and what you do differently from here.

The video sessions and study guide are by Faith Driven Entrepreneur, taught by Henry Kaestner and J.D. Greear. We use their material as a resource. 07Facilitators

Facilitated, not lectured.

Kelly and Alex facilitate. That is a deliberate distinction and one we hold to: their job is to steer the conversation, keep everyone heard, and hold the group’s culture — not to stand at the front and teach. The teaching comes from the video; the wisdom comes from around the table.

Kelly

Facilitator

Kelly leads from one conviction: what she leads isn’t hers. It’s on loan, and she’s the one accountable for how it’s stewarded in the meantime. She started this group because she wanted a room where leaders keep each other honest about that — people who call each other back to leading like Christ does, cheer for each other’s work instead of competing with it, and never let each other forget whose it actually is.

Alex

Facilitator

Alex leads from the same conviction Kelly does: everything we lead — the people, the resources, the decisions — is borrowed, not owned. He wanted a group where that isn’t just a nice idea but something people actually hold each other to: leading the way God asks, backing each other’s work instead of guarding their own turf, and staying honest about who’s really in charge.

08Questions

Before you reach out.

Who is this group for?

Christians who lead — and Christians who want to learn to — and who want their faith to actually govern how they do it, not sit beside it. Business owners, managers, ministry and nonprofit leaders, and anyone stewarding people, money, or influence are all welcome. If you carry decisions you want to make faithfully, and you want peers who will pray about them and then hold you to what you said, you’re the person we built this for.

How long is the commitment?

Ten weeks, one hour a week, in person — part of Church by the Glades’ fall lifegroup semester. Eight of those weeks are the video sessions; the first and last run about thirty minutes longer, for introductions at the start and reflection at the end.

A defined end date is deliberate. You are agreeing to ten weeks, not to something open-ended you have to find a polite way out of.

What will we actually study?

A video study from Faith Driven Entrepreneur — eight sessions taught by Henry Kaestner and J.D. Greear, each paired with a real story from someone living out their faith at work. The session list is above. We use their material as a resource; we’re an independent lifegroup, not one of their groups.

We watch each video together in the room rather than as homework. Nobody has to arrive prepared, and nobody has to pretend they watched it.

Do I need to own a business to join?

No. Business owners are welcome, but so are managers, team leads, ministry and nonprofit leaders, and anyone who wants to grow into leading well. What matters is that you’re responsible for something — people, resources, a mission — and want to steward it faithfully.

What is expected of me?

We set our own ground rules in week one, but four come with the curriculum and we keep them:

  • Show up to at least 80% of the sessions. Community doesn’t survive inconsistency.
  • Confidentiality, and the vulnerability that earns. What is said here stays here.
  • Respect for different theological traditions. We come from various church backgrounds and look for Gospel unity across them.
  • Group time stays on the study and each other. We keep the hour on the conversation, not on side agendas.
How do I join?

There’s no form here on purpose. Registration runs through Church by the Glades with the rest of the fall lifegroups, and the facilitators add members personally so the group stays a room of people who actually know each other.

Two ways to start: sign up through Church by the Glades and ask for the Jesus Is My CEO group, or reach out to Kelly or Alex directly using the details below. Expect a real conversation before the first session — that’s the point.

Is there a cost?

There’s no cost to join — no dues, nothing to buy to be part of the group. If that ever changes, you’ll hear it directly from a facilitator before it affects you, never as a surprise.

How do the Questions of the Week work?

Each session ends with a couple of questions. You write your answer down, we collect them, and the facilitators post the questions on the Questions of the Week section of this site.

Members of the group can submit an answer through the Google Form linked there. A facilitator reviews every submission, and the ones we post go up on the page for the whole group to read. The idea is that someone who has been through something similar can speak wisdom into it — a way to help each other get to good answers between sessions.

What happens when the ten weeks are up?

The relationships are meant to outlast the study. In the final session we talk about what comes next as a group — whether to keep meeting, and who is ready to facilitate the next cohort.

That last part is built into the curriculum on purpose: the people it works on are expected to go and lead it for someone else.

What if I’m not sure I belong in a group like this?

Come and see. Most people arrive unsure — either they think their business is too small to be worth discussing or too complicated to bring into a faith conversation. Neither has been true yet. Reach out and we’ll talk about whether this is the right table for you.
