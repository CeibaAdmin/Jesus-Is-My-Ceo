## The email

Hi Cassidy,

[KELLY'S CONNECTION TO CBG — e.g. "Alex and I have been at Church by the Glades for six
years, and I've served on the [TEAM] team."] I wanted to bring you an idea for the fall
before the lifegroup schedule gets locked in.

Alex and I would like to lead a group for the business owners and entrepreneurs in our
church. We're both in that world — [KELLY'S BUSINESS] and [ALEX'S BUSINESS] — and we keep
noticing the same thing: owners carry decisions nobody else in their life fully
understands, and they're often the least connected people in the room on a Sunday.
They'll take a hiring problem or a cash-flow scare to a consultant before they'd bring it
to their church.

**What it is.** Ten weeks, in person, working through the Faith Driven Entrepreneur
video study — eight video sessions taught by J.D. Greear and Henry Kaestner,
each paired with a story from a working entrepreneur. About an hour a week: open in
prayer, watch the teaching together, talk about it honestly, pray over each other before
we leave. No homework, which matters for this crowd.

The centre of it is stewardship. Session two is called "God Owns My Business," and the
question it puts to the room is the one we want people sitting in for ten weeks: if God
is the actual owner and I'm the steward, what changes about how I decide things on
Monday? It's a direct challenge to the "God gets ten percent and I own the rest"
instinct a lot of us have without ever saying out loud.

We're using the Faith Driven Entrepreneur video study as our material, so there's a
proven structure behind us and not just enthusiasm — a clear eight-session arc and a
facilitator guide we're following closely. To be clear, we're an independent Church by the
Glades lifegroup: we use their study as a resource but we're not registered with,
affiliated with, or a group of Faith Driven Entrepreneur. We're not improvising this.

**One thing I want to name up front.** Put a room of business owners together for ten
weeks and they will start trusting each other enough to refer work. We think that's good
fruit and we're not going to pretend it won't happen. But we are not running a networking
group, and FDE draws that line themselves in the facilitator guide:

> "Weekly group time will focus on content and relationship. Networking and any business
> development can occur outside of the group time if desired - we're here to build
> transformational relationships."

That's the rule we're holding to. The hour is for the study and for each other. Anything
else happens on someone's own time.

**Logistics as we picture it:**

- Ten weeks, in person, aligned to the fall semester — starting [COHORT START DATE]
- [DAY/TIME]
- [LOCATION]
- Open to any owner or operator, men and women, industries deliberately mixed
- Around [CAPACITY] people — small enough that everyone actually talks

**What we're asking:** would you approve this as a fall lifegroup? And whatever comes
next on your end — an application, a leader interview, coaching — point us at it and
we'll do it.

I've attached FDE's facilitator guide so you can see the session structure and the
expectations we'd set with the group in week one. Happy to grab coffee if it's easier to
talk through than to read.

Thanks for considering it,

Kelly [KELLY'S LAST NAME]
(786) 361-7028 · jesusismyceolifegroup@gmail.com

## Before this goes out

Blocking — the email doesn't work without these:

- [ ] **Cassidy's last name**, and confirm the spelling of her first name.
- [ ] **Kelly's and Alex's connection to CBG** — how long they've attended, anything
      they've served in. This is the most persuasive line in the email for a lifegroups
      director, and it's the one thing only they can supply. Don't send without it.
- [ ] **Which business belongs to whom** — Ceiba Accounting and The Balanced Coach still
      aren't matched to Kelly and Alex.
- [ ] **Day, time, location, start date, capacity.** She can't approve a slot that was
      never proposed. Better to suggest something concrete and be corrected than to
      leave her guessing.
- [ ] **Who is sending it.** Drafted in Kelly's voice; if Alex sends it, swap the
      first-person references and the signature.

Worth deciding:

- [ ] **Whether to mention cost.** If the participant study guide has a per-person price,
      Cassidy will want to know — churches are sensitive about barriers to entry.
      Currently omitted to keep the email short. If it's a real number, add one line to
      the logistics list rather than burying it.
- [ ] **Whether to offer the website.** Held back deliberately — it still has unfilled
      placeholders (meeting day/time, location, the Questions of the Week Google Form link).
      Once the placeholders are resolved, sending her the link makes a natural second
      touchpoint after she replies.

## Why the email is built this way

What Cassidy is weighing, and where each concern gets answered:

| Her likely concern | Where it's handled |
| --- | --- |
| Is this a networking club using the church for lead generation? | Named head-on, mid-email, with FDE's own rule quoted. The biggest risk to a yes, so it's raised before she has to. |
| Is the curriculum theologically sound? | J.D. Greear's name, stated plainly. A pastor she'll almost certainly recognise does more work here than any assurance we could write. |
| Are the leaders ready and accountable? | A proven, structured curriculum with a facilitator guide we follow, plus whatever accountability CBG's own lifegroup process provides. |
| Does it fill a real gap? | The opening paragraph: owners are isolated and under-shepherded. |
| Is it exclusive? | "Any owner or operator, men and women, industries deliberately mixed." |

Two deliberate omissions. The guide's "cameras on" expectation is Zoom-specific and
doesn't apply to an in-person group, so it's left out rather than explained. And the
email asks for approval without ever implying it's been given — nothing in it can be
read as claiming church endorsement.
